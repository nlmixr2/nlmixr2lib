## Model and source

- Citation: Ma H, Wang H, Sove RJ, Wang J, Giragossian C, Popel AS.
  Combination therapy with T cell engager and PD-L1 blockade enhances
  the antitumor potency of T cells as predicted by a QSP model. J
  Immunother Cancer. 2020;8(2):e001141. <doi:10.1136/jitc-2020-001141>.
- Article: <https://doi.org/10.1136/jitc-2020-001141>

This is a deterministic quantitative systems pharmacology (QSP) model of
microsatellite-stable (MSS) metastatic colorectal cancer treated with
the anti-PD-L1 antibody atezolizumab, the CEA x CD3 bispecific T cell
engager (TCE) cibisatamab, or both. It belongs to the Popel-laboratory
immuno-oncology QSP family: the backbone is the non-small cell lung
cancer model of Jafarnejad et al. (AAPS J 2019), the TCE module is that
of Ma et al. (AAPS J 2020;22:85, the cibisatamab monotherapy paper), and
this paper adds three updates described in its Supplementary
Information:

- **PD-L2 with a weight delta** (Supplementary Eqs 6-11): the number of
  PD-1/ligand complexes that drives T cell inhibition is
  `PD1_PDL1 + delta * PD1_PDL2`, in the Teff-cancer cell synapse and in
  the Teff-APC synapse. Atezolizumab blocks PD-L1 but not PD-L2.
- **PD-L1/PD-L2 on antigen-presenting cells limit T cell proliferation
  in the lymph node** (Supplementary Eq 12): the proliferation of newly
  activated T cells is multiplied by `1 - H_PD1_APC`, so atezolizumab
  acts during priming as well as in the tumour.
- **Teff killing rate combining the checkpoint and the TCE**
  (Supplementary Eqs 13-14): basal Teff killing inhibited by `H_PD1_C1`,
  plus TCE-driven killing proportional to a Hill function of the
  CEA-cibisatamab-CD3 trimers.

The later Anbari 2023 platform
(`Anbari_2023_atezolizumab_cibisatamab_qsp`) extends this model with
myeloid-derived suppressor cells, T helper cells and cytokines; it is a
separate model with its own parameter tables.

The authors built the model in SimBiology and published its SBML export
as Supplementary file 2. The packaged model is a translation of that
export (73 species, 105 reactions, 22 repeated-assignment rules, 12
initial assignments and one event), with the baseline values printed in
Supplementary Tables S2 and S3 used wherever the export carries a
different number (see *Assumptions and deviations*).

There are no etas and no residual-error model: the authors did not fit
inter-individual variability. They generated 2000 virtual patients by
Latin hypercube sampling of the Table S3 ranges. The model is packaged
as the typical (Table S3 baseline) individual.

### How the translation works

SimBiology converts units implicitly; rxode2 does not. Every value was
converted to one consistent unit system – amounts in counts (the export
defines 1 cell = 1 molecule = 1/Avogadro mol), volumes in litres, areas
in dm^2, time in days – and each of the 105 rate laws, 22 rules and 12
initial assignments was checked by a dimensional-analysis pass that
requires every rate to reduce to amount/time. The ODE states hold
*amounts*, `q_<compartment>_<species>` (compartment and species names as
in the export); the `x_<compartment>_<species>` variables are the
SimBiology species values (concentrations for species stored as
concentrations). Integrating amounts is what SimBiology does internally
and handles the growing tumour volume without a dilution term. The
reaction rates `v1`-`v105` are numbered in the export’s reaction order,
and each line of the model carries the reaction it encodes.

The two antibodies are held in **nmol**, so dose records are in nmol:
atezolizumab into `q_V_C_atezolizumab`, cibisatamab into `q_V_C_cibis`
(IV boluses). Every other state is a count.

Model outputs: `tumour_diameter` (cm, from the total tumour volume
assuming a sphere), `Cc_atezolizumab` and `Cc_cibisatamab` (central
concentrations, nmol/L), and the tumour-infiltrating cell densities
`teff_density` and `treg_density` (cells per mL of tumour). The Hill
terms `H_PD1_C1`, `H_PD1_APC`, `H_CEA_C1_T1` and `H_CEA_C1_T0` are also
returned.

#### The pre-treatment phase

As in the paper, a simulation starts from a single cancer cell with no T
cells anywhere and the tumour grows until it reaches the pre-treatment
diameter; therapy then starts. The pre-treatment phase has no doses, so
the untreated model is solved once to find the time at which the tumour
reaches that diameter, and the first dose is scheduled at that time.

## Population

The virtual population is described in the Results (“In silico VCT
outcomes”). 2000 virtual patients were generated by Latin hypercube
sampling of the parameters in Table S3 (tumour growth rate, initial
tumour diameter 0.5-5 cm, tumour mutational burden, PD-1, PD-L1, PD-L2,
CEA and CD3 expression, binding constants and cross-arm binding
efficiencies). Patients were screened on tumour diameter, blood T cell
density, activated T cell density in the tumour and the Teff/Treg ratio,
leaving 1312 (atezolizumab 1200 mg Q3W), 1325 (cibisatamab 60 mg QW) and
1299 (combination) plausible patients. At day 400 the simulated
objective response rates (ORR, RECIST 1.1) were 8.2, 5.2 and 11.2
percent, against 6 percent (cibisatamab, NCT02324257) and 12 percent
(combination, NCT02650713) in the clinic (Table S4).

## Simulation set-up

``` r

# Dosing conversion for the vignette (not a model parameter). The paper gives
# doses in mg and no molar masses. Atezolizumab 145 kDa is the value used by the
# same group's atezolizumab QSP (Wang 2021, Supplementary Methods). No source
# gives the cibisatamab molar mass; 145 kDa is used as a carrier, see
# "Assumptions and deviations".
mw_kda <- c(atezolizumab = 145, cibisatamab = 145)
dose_nmol <- function(mg, drug) mg / (mw_kda[[drug]] * 1e3) * 1e6
atezo_nmol <- dose_nmol(1200, "atezolizumab")
cibis_nmol <- dose_nmol(60, "cibisatamab")

# method = "cvode" (SUNDIALS BDF). The stiff growth phase takes ~7 s per solve
# with lsoda and ~0.4 s with cvode; the final tumour diameters agree to about
# 1e-4 (relative).
SOLVE <- function(ev, params = NULL) {
  rxode2::rxSolve(mod, ev, params = params, method = "cvode", maxsteps = 1e6, returnType = "data.frame")
}

# Time at which the untreated tumour reaches diameter d0 (cm); NA if it never
# does (the immune system controls it, so the virtual patient has no tumour).
grow_to <- function(params = NULL, d0 = 3) {
  g <- SOLVE(rxode2::et(c(0, seq(1000, 9000, by = 10))), params = params)
  if (max(g$tumour_diameter) < d0) {
    return(NA_real_)
  }
  i <- which(g$tumour_diameter >= d0)[1]
  approx(g$tumour_diameter[(i - 1):i], g$time[(i - 1):i], d0)$y
}

# Treat from time ts for 400 days.
treat <- function(ts, atezo = FALSE, cibis = FALSE, params = NULL, obs = seq(0, 400, by = 4)) {
  ev <- rxode2::et(ts + obs)
  if (atezo) ev <- rxode2::et(ev, time = ts, amt = atezo_nmol, cmt = "q_V_C_atezolizumab", ii = 21, addl = 19)
  if (cibis) ev <- rxode2::et(ev, time = ts, amt = cibis_nmol, cmt = "q_V_C_cibis", ii = 7, addl = 57)
  s <- SOLVE(ev, params = params)
  s$day <- s$time - ts
  s
}

arm_def <- list(
  "Untreated" = c(atezo = FALSE, cibis = FALSE),
  "Atezolizumab" = c(atezo = TRUE, cibis = FALSE),
  "Cibisatamab" = c(atezo = FALSE, cibis = TRUE),
  "Combination" = c(atezo = TRUE, cibis = TRUE)
)
# Percentage change in tumour diameter at day 400 for each treated arm
pct400 <- function(params = NULL, d0 = 3, arms = names(arm_def)[-1]) {
  ts <- grow_to(params, d0)
  vapply(arms, function(a) {
    s <- treat(ts, arm_def[[a]][["atezo"]], arm_def[[a]][["cibis"]], params, obs = c(0, 400))
    100 * (s$tumour_diameter[s$day == 400] / d0 - 1)
  }, numeric(1))
}
```

Supplementary Figure S1 shows the atezolizumab plasma profile after a
single 1200 mg dose, simulated and measured, with the measured points
lying on the simulated curve. The points below were digitised by the
maintainers. Both antibodies distribute in the same four compartments
and are not consumed by their binding reactions (in the export the drug
appears only in the rate law, never as a reactant), so their PK is
linear and independent of the tumour; the doses are given at time zero.

**Supplementary Figure S1 is not reproduced by the deposited
atezolizumab PK.** The figure starts at 358 ug/mL, which implies a
central volume of about 3.3 L (the same 3.3 L follows from the 1, 3, 10
and 15 mg/kg curves at 70 kg), then shows a distribution phase and a
slow terminal phase. The export’s central compartment is 5 L
(`vol_V_C`), its central-peripheral flow `q_P_atezolizumab` is 7e-8 mL/s
(6e-6 L/day, so there is effectively no peripheral distribution) and its
elimination rate `k_cl_atezolizumab` is 0.003/h; the simulated profile
therefore starts at 240 ug/mL and declines almost mono-exponentially
with a 9.6-day half-life, to 53 against 81 ug/mL at day 21 (starred
rows); the 0-21 day AUC is within 12 percent only because the simulated
curve lies above the figure between days 4 and 11. The Supplementary
text says the QSP PK was fitted to a two-compartment model of the
clinical data, so the curves in Figure S1 are most likely that
two-compartment fit rather than the deposited QSP. No value was changed:
the PK parameters are those of the export, and Table S2 does not print
them.

The baseline patient has a tumour mutational burden of 10
tumour-specific T cell clones, so few effector T cells are in the tumour
when therapy starts (under 1000 per mL) and neither drug can control a 3
cm tumour. The mechanistic effects are nevertheless visible:
atezolizumab lowers the PD-1 inhibition term in the tumour synapse
(`H_PD1_C1`, from 0.999 to 0.75 – PD-L2, which atezolizumab does not
block, keeps it high) and in the lymph-node synapse, and raises the Teff
density; cibisatamab occupies the TCE Hill term.

## CEA expression and tumour growth rate (Figure 2B)

Under the combination, CEA expression on cancer cells is negatively and
the tumour growth rate positively correlated with tumour volume (Figure
2B). Both are checked one at a time at TMB 500, where the reference
patient responds to the combination.

## PD-L2 weight delta (Supplementary Eqs 10-11)

The authors introduced delta because with PD-L2 tied to PD-L1 through a
fixed ratio, responders appeared to carry more PD-L2; sampling delta on
0-1 changed the atezolizumab ORR from 8.2 to 9.3 percent. For a single
patient the weight can matter much more, because PD-L2 is the part of
the checkpoint signal that atezolizumab leaves untouched:

## Mass conservation in the synapses

Receptor and ligand totals in each synapse are conserved by the binding
reactions (the antibodies are not consumed). This is an exact identity
of the ODE system, so it is checked tightly on the combination arm.

``` r

ini_val <- function(nm) mod$iniDf$est[mod$iniDf$name == nm]
```

## Assumptions and deviations

- **Values that differ between the printed tables and the SBML export.**
  The printed baselines are used, with the export’s value noted in each
  parameter’s comment:
  - `k_Treg` = 1/day (export: 2/day, outside the Table S3 range 0.1-1);
  - `n_T1_clones` (tumour mutational burden) = 10 (export: 80);
  - `C_PDL1_total` = 160000 per cell (Tables S2 and S3; export: 400000,
    outside the Table S3 range 1-160000);
  - `r_PDL2C` and `r_PDL2APC` = 0.065, the ratio of the Table S2 PD-L2
    and PD-L1 totals (10400/160000 on cancer cells, 104000/1600000 on
    APCs; export: 0.05). Table S3 prints a baseline of 0.1, outside its
    own 0-0.07 range and the text’s 0.07 upper limit, so the Table S2
    totals are taken;
  - `T1_CD3_total` and `T0_CD3_total` = 61000 per cell (Table S3, and Ma
    2020a Table S2; export: 62000);
  - `Chi_CEA_cibis` = lambda / `dT_syn` with lambda = 1000 (Table S3,
    and Ma 2020a Tables S2 and S3; the export’s 0.3333/nm corresponds to
    lambda = 1);
  - the PD-1 complex Hill constant is Table S2’s 250 complexes per
    synapse (`K_PD1_PDLX`); the export’s `PD1_50` of 6 molecules/um^2
    over the 37.8 um^2 synapse is 226.8.

  The scenario above shows that the export’s values do not produce
  responders.
- **PD-L2 weight.** The export has no delta; `delta_PDL2` = 1 reproduces
  it, and the paper sampled delta on 0-1 only in a sensitivity rerun.
- **Atezolizumab PK.** Not changed, although it does not reproduce
  Supplementary Figure S1 (see the PK section).
- **Exported formulas kept as they are.** The atezolizumab concentration
  that binds PD-L1 in the Teff-APC (lymph node) synapse is the tumour
  concentration, divided by the lymph-node available fraction of
  cibisatamab (`gamma_LN_cibis`, numerically equal to
  `gamma_LN_atezolizumab` = 0.2); in the Teff-cancer cell synapse it is
  divided by the central available fraction `gamma_C_atezolizumab`
  rather than the tumour porosity of Supplementary Eq 3. TCE-driven
  killing through the Treg-cancer cell synapse multiplies the Teff count
  (`k_C_BTreg` term of `v24`). These are the export’s rate laws and are
  reproduced exactly.
- **Anti-PD-1 states.** The export carries anti-PD-1 binding states and
  parameters from the backbone model with the anti-PD-1 concentration
  `aPD1` set to 0; they are kept and stay at zero.
- **Unused export parameters** (`A_syn`, `SA_APC`, `T_syn`,
  `initial_tumour_diameter`, `kon_CD3_cibis2`, `kon_CEA_cibis2`) are not
  carried; `PD1_50` is replaced by `K_PD1_PDLX`.
- **Cancer-cell elimination event.** SimBiology resets the cancer cell
  count to zero below 0.9 cells. rxode2 has no state-reset events, so
  proliferation is switched off below 0.9 cells (`c1_alive`) and the
  remaining fraction decays through the death terms.
- **Molar masses.** No source gives them. Atezolizumab uses 145 kDa (the
  same group’s Wang 2021 atezolizumab QSP); cibisatamab uses 145 kDa as
  a carrier only. The cibisatamab dose in nmol therefore carries that
  assumption; Ma 2020a reports no ORR difference between 60 and 600 mg
  weekly, so the efficacy results are insensitive to it.
- **Virtual cohort.** The paper’s ORRs come from 2000 Latin hypercube
  samples whose sampling distributions are not stated beyond the Table
  S3 ranges, so they are not re-simulated; the vignette checks the
  reported directions of effect on the baseline patient instead.
- **State names.** States keep the SimBiology `compartment.species`
  naming (`q_V_T_C1`, `q_syn_T1_C1_PD1`, …) so each one can be traced to
  the export; they are not mapped onto the library’s PK compartment
  names.
