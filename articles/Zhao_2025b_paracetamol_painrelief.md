# Paracetamol pain relief (Zhao 2025)

## Model and source

- Citation: Zhao S, Zhang L (2025). Evaluation of the PK/PD Changes on
  MASLD-Related Population - An Example From Simultaneous Acetaminophen
  Parent-Metabolite PBPK/PD Modeling. CPT Pharmacometrics Syst Pharmacol
  15(1):e70161. <doi:10.1002/psp4.70161>.
- Description: Direct sigmoid Emax (Hill) pharmacodynamic model relating
  the plasma concentration of paracetamol (acetaminophen) to the pain
  relief score (0-10 scale, higher = more relief) in adults with chronic
  pain (Zhao 2025, the PD layer of a GastroPlus parent-metabolite
  PBPK/PD model for MASLD-related populations). The PD relationship was
  benchmarked against the plasma concentration / pain relief data of
  Shinoda 2007 (Japanese chronic-pain patients given a 1 g oral tablet).
  Only this PD layer is reproduced here: the paracetamol and
  five-metabolite PBPK layers are GastroPlus platform models whose
  tissue partition coefficients (Lukacova method), organ volumes and
  blood flows, per-tissue enzyme amounts and MASLD-population
  physiologies are software-database outputs that the paper never
  prints, so they are not reproducible from the publication (see the
  vignette ‘Assumptions and deviations’). Paracetamol plasma
  concentration is therefore supplied by the user as the canonical
  effect-site covariate CEFFECT rather than generated by a PK model in
  this file. No inter-individual variability and no residual-error model
  are reported, so both are absent / fixed at zero.
- Article: <https://doi.org/10.1002/psp4.70161> (open access,
  PMC12823458)

Zhao 2025 builds a GastroPlus whole-body PBPK model of paracetamol
(acetaminophen, APAP) together with five metabolites (glucuronide,
sulfate, NAPQI, cysteine and mercapturate conjugates) in healthy adults,
extrapolates it to overweight, obese, NAFLD, NASH and cirrhotic
(Child-Pugh A/B/C) virtual populations using the platform’s built-in
disease physiologies, and finally links the predicted plasma paracetamol
concentration to a 0-10 pain relief score with a direct sigmoid Emax
model.

**Only the last component, the pain relief PD layer, is packaged in
nlmixr2lib.** The PBPK layers cannot be reproduced from the publication
(see [Assumptions and deviations](#assumptions-and-deviations)). The PD
layer is fully specified by four printed numbers and is self-contained
once a plasma paracetamol concentration is supplied through the
`CEFFECT` covariate column.

Note that `Zhao_2025_paracetamol` in this package is a different paper
(a population PK model in spinal muscular atrophy, Br J Clin Pharmacol
2025); the `2025b` in this model’s name only separates the two.

## Population

The PD relationship was benchmarked on the plasma concentration and pain
relief data of Shinoda 2007 (Biol Pharm Bull 30:157-161), a study of
Japanese patients with chronic pain given a single 1 g oral paracetamol
tablet (Zhao 2025 Methods, “The PK/PD Model on the MASLD-Related
Population”, and supplementary Table S3). Zhao 2025 does not restate the
size or demographics of that cohort, so they are left unreported in the
model metadata. The PD model was then applied, unchanged, to the
PBPK-predicted plasma profiles of the normal-weight, overweight, obese,
non-NAFLD, NAFLD, NASH, healthy and cirrhosis Child-Pugh A/B/C virtual
populations.

### Definitional checks

These checks use the model on its own printed parameters, so they are
exact and a transcription error in any of the four values breaks them.

``` r

score_at <- function(conc) {
  d <- data.frame(id = 1L, time = seq_along(conc) - 1, evid = 0L, CEFFECT = conc)
  as.data.frame(rxSolve(mod, d))$score
}
emax <- 3.898
ec50 <- 1.612
hill <- 1.36
checks <- data.frame(
  check = c(
    "score at C = 0 equals E0",
    "score at C = EC50 equals Emax / 2",
    "score at C = 1000 ug/mL approaches Emax",
    "score at 2 x EC50, closed form"
  ),
  model = score_at(c(0, ec50, 1000, 2 * ec50)),
  expected = c(0, emax / 2, emax * 1000^hill / (ec50^hill + 1000^hill),
               emax * 2^hill / (1 + 2^hill))
)
knitr::kable(checks, digits = 5)
```

### Peak scores stated in the paper

Zhao 2025 reports that, from normal weight to obese, the predicted peak
pain relief score falls “from 3.15 to 2.63”. Because the model is a
direct Emax on plasma concentration, each peak score corresponds to
exactly one peak plasma concentration, obtained by inverting the Hill
equation: `C = EC50 * (E / (Emax - E))^(1 / hill)`.

``` r

inv <- data.frame(
  population = c("normal weight", "obese"),
  peak_score = c(3.15, 2.63)
) |>
  mutate(implied_cmax_ug_mL = ec50 * (peak_score / (emax - peak_score))^(1 / hill))
inv$model_score_at_implied_cmax <- score_at(inv$implied_cmax_ug_mL)
inv |>
  dplyr::rename(
    "Population" = population,
    "Peak score (paper)" = peak_score,
    "Implied plasma Cmax (ug/mL)" = implied_cmax_ug_mL,
    "Model score at that Cmax" = model_score_at_implied_cmax
  ) |>
  knitr::kable(digits = 3)
```

| Population | Peak score (paper) | Implied plasma Cmax (ug/mL) | Model score at that Cmax |
|:---|---:|---:|---:|
| normal weight | 3.15 | 4.640 | 3.15 |
| obese | 2.63 | 2.756 | 2.63 |

``` r

stopifnot(all(abs(inv$model_score_at_implied_cmax - inv$peak_score) < 1e-8))
```

The implied peak concentrations, 4.6 and 2.8 ug/mL, lie inside the
0-8000 ng/mL concentration axis of the normal-weight / overweight /
obese panel of Figure 5, and the obese population’s lower value is
consistent with the paper’s statement that obesity lowers APAP peak
concentrations. The supplement reports a PBPK-simulated plasma Cmax of
about 10000 ng/mL for the 1 g tablet benchmark (Figure S7); at 10 ug/mL
the model gives a score of 3.6, close to the plateau of about 3.6-3.8
visible in the control-group curves of Figure 5.

## Simulation: driving the PD model from a population PK model

In use, `CEFFECT` comes from a paracetamol PK model. Here the plasma
profile after a single 1 g fasted tablet in a 70 kg, 176 cm man is taken
from the library’s `Morse_2022_acetaminophen` popPK model (healthy
adults, the same 1 g tablet dose as the Shinoda benchmark) and fed into
the pain relief model. This illustrates the workflow; it does not
reproduce the GastroPlus profiles of Zhao 2025, whose peak-effect times
(0.49-0.97 h across the populations) depend on the unreproducible PBPK
layer.

``` r

  ev$HT <- 176
  ev$SEXF <- 0
```

### NCA of the driving profile and the direct-effect identity

For a direct (no effect-compartment) Emax model, the peak score occurs
at the plasma Tmax and equals the Emax function evaluated at the plasma
Cmax. PKNCA supplies the driver’s Cmax and Tmax.


### Variability in the driving PK

in the predicted score below comes from the Morse 2022 PK variability
(200 simulated adults with the same covariates).

## Assumptions and deviations

- **Only the PD layer is packaged.** The paracetamol and five-metabolite
  PBPK layers were built in GastroPlus. Their tissue partition
  coefficients are given only as a method name (Lukacova), enzyme Vmax
  values are expressed per mg of enzyme with the per-tissue enzyme
  amounts left to the software, organ volumes and blood flows come from
  the software’s PEAR physiology generator, and the overweight / obese /
  NAFLD / NASH / cirrhosis physiologies are the platform’s preset
  populations used without modification (Discussion). None of these are
  printed, so the PBPK model cannot be reproduced from the publication.
  The paper’s printed Vss (68.46 L) and total clearance (21.18 L/h) are
  noncompartmental summaries of the observed intravenous studies used as
  benchmarks, not parameters of the PBPK model, and a compartmental
  model built from them would not carry the paper’s MASLD-population
  predictions. They are recorded in the `CEFFECT` covariate notes for
  provenance.
- **Plasma is the effect site.** The paper relates the score directly to
  plasma concentration with no effect compartment; its supplement argues
  that plasma is a reasonable surrogate for unbound brain concentration,
  because the simulated profiles peak 0.07 h apart.
- **Origin of the PD parameters.** The paper calls E0, EC50, Emax and
  the Hill coefficient “the individual PD parameters” obtained with the
  GastroPlus PD module against the Shinoda 2007 benchmark data, and
  gives no standard errors. They are carried exactly as printed; E0 = 0
  is the bottom of the score scale and is fixed.
- **No variability or residual error.** No between-subject variability
  or residual-error model is reported for the PD layer; the residual SD
  is fixed at zero. Users fitting this model to individual data should
  free it.
- **Benchmark cohort not described.** Zhao 2025 does not restate the
  size, sex mix or body weight of the Shinoda 2007 cohort, so those
  population fields are left unreported.
- **Figure 5 not digitised.** Figure 5 is a 3-D rendering of score
  against time and concentration, which cannot be read reliably; the
  checks above use the peak scores and concentrations stated in the text
  instead.
- **Driving PK in this vignette.** The simulations use the library’s
  Morse 2022 popPK model as the source of `CEFFECT`, which is a
  demonstration of the intended workflow and not part of Zhao 2025.
